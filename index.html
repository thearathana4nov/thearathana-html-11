    Khmer New Year, known in Khmer as Choul Chnam Thmey, is one of Cambodia’s most important and joyful celebrations. It usually takes place in mid-April, when families welcome the traditional New Year and enjoy a break from work and school. The festival lasts for three days, and each day has its own customs and meaning. People clean and decorate their homes, prepare special food, and visit temples to pray, make offerings, and listen to Buddhist teachings. Many families build small sand stupas in temple courtyards, believing that this brings good fortune and honors the Buddha. A popular tradition is to gently pour scented water over Buddha statues and the hands of elders as a sign of respect, gratitude, and a wish for health and happiness. Younger people also ask their parents and grandparents for blessings. In towns and villages, the holiday is filled with music, dancing, games, and friendly gatherings. People may play traditional games such as Chol Chhoung, in which teams toss a scarf and try to catch it, or dance the lively circle dance called Romvong. Families often share dishes such as rice, noodles, fruit, and sweets, while visitors return to their hometowns to spend time with relatives. Although customs vary from place to place, kindness, respect, and togetherness are at the heart of the celebration. Khmer New Year is also a time to reflect on the past year and hope for a better one ahead. The festival connects Cambodians with their culture and Buddhist traditions, while giving younger generations a chance to learn from their elders. For many people, the happiest moments are simple ones: sharing a meal, laughing with family, visiting a temple, or dancing with neighbors. Through these traditions, Khmer New Year welcomes fresh beginnings and strengthens the bonds between families and communities.
